Free & Open-Weight AI Models: Complete Late-2026 Guide

The open-weight AI landscape has changed dramatically since February. New model generations have shipped from every major family, licensing has shifted (notably Gemma going Apache 2.0), OpenAI released its first open-weight LLMs in years, and entirely new families entered the field. This is a full refresh of the February 2026 guide — updated models, corrected licenses, and new recommendations.

A note on terminology: most models here are open-weight, not open-source. You get downloadable weights you can run, fine-tune, and build on — but not the training data or full training pipeline. Licenses vary more than marketing suggests; the table below gives the real terms per family.

What changed since February 2026

  • Llama 4 (Scout/Maverick, MoE) replaced Llama 3.x as Meta's current line
  • Gemma 4 moved to Apache 2.0 — a major licensing improvement over Gemma 3's custom terms
  • DeepSeek V4 / V4.1-Flash pushed efficiency further: 4x less KV-cache memory via a new encoder-decoder architecture
  • Qwen3 expanded into a full ladder: dense, MoE, coder, vision, and omni variants (plus a 397B Qwen3.5 MoE)
  • OpenAI released gpt-oss (20B/120B MoE, Apache 2.0) — its first open-weight language models since GPT-2
  • New families: Microsoft's Phi (small-model specialist, MIT), Moonshot's Kimi K3 (2.8T-parameter MoE), Z.ai's GLM-4.7 (coding-focused, MIT)

1. Qwen3 (Alibaba)

Overview

Qwen is the broadest open-weight family: the original Qwen3 series spans 0.6B–235B across dense and MoE architectures, with dedicated coder, vision-language, and omni variants. Qwen3-Coder is officially billed as the Qwen team's "most agentic code model to date." The newer Qwen3.5 series adds a 397B MoE flagship.

Key Specifications

  • Sizes: 0.6B to 235B (dense + MoE); Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (397B total / 17B active) is the largest
  • Variants: Qwen3 (general), Qwen3-Coder, Qwen3-VL, Qwen3-Omni
  • Strengths: Agentic coding (Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B-Instruct), 119 languages and dialects, 1M-token context via Qwen3-Coder extrapolation
  • License: Apache 2.0 — all Qwen3 models, per the official technical report

2. Llama 4 (Meta)

Overview

Meta's current generation: Scout (109B total) and Maverick (~400B total), both MoE with 17B active parameters. Llama remains the default choice for the largest fine-tuning ecosystem — but the license carries restrictions that matter at commercial scale: gated downloads, a >700M monthly-active-user threshold requiring a separate Meta license, an Acceptable Use Policy, "Built with Llama" attribution, and EU restrictions on the multimodal models.

Key Specifications

  • Models: Llama 4 Scout (109B total / 17B active), Llama 4 Maverick (~400B total / 17B active), MoE
  • Strengths: Largest community, most fine-tunes and integrations, natively multimodal
  • License: Custom Llama 4 Community License — gated downloads, usage thresholds apply. Read it before commercial deployment.

Download Links

  • Official Meta LLaMA
  • Hugging Face
  • Ollama

3. DeepSeek (V4 / R1)

Overview

DeepSeek continues to set the efficiency bar. V4-Pro (1.6T total / 49B active) and V4-Flash (284B / 13B active) ship with 1M-token context and thinking modes; V4.1-Flash's Causal Encoder-Decoder architecture cut KV-cache memory ~4x versus V4-Flash (890 bytes/token). R1 remains the reference open reasoning model (671B / 37B active). All recent releases are MIT-licensed — the safest default for commercial use.

Key Specifications

  • DeepSeek-V4-Pro / V4-Flash: Flagship MoE, 1M context, agentic coding SOTA among open models
  • DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash: Efficiency-optimized — ~4x less KV-cache memory than V4-Flash
  • DeepSeek-R1: Advanced reasoning, math, logic — comparable to OpenAI's o1-class at release
  • Strengths: Reasoning quality near the closed frontier, cost efficiency, permissive MIT terms
  • License: MIT across recent releases (R1, V4, V4.1). The older V3 line used a custom DeepSeek license.

Download Links

  • Official DeepSeek
  • Hugging Face

4. Mistral AI

Overview

Mistral's lineup splits by license: Small and Large lines are Apache 2.0, while Medium ships under a Modified MIT license with a monthly revenue cap ($20M consolidated revenue in the preceding month). Still the efficiency-first European option, and the natural pick for EU data-residency requirements — La Plateforme hosts data in the EU by default. Codestral's 22B weights are non-commercial (MNLP-0.1 license).

Key Specifications

  • Sizes: 3B (Ministral/Voxtral edge models) to 675B (Mistral Large 3, 41B active MoE)
  • Models: Mistral Small 4 (119B MoE, Apache 2.0), Mistral Medium 3.5 (128B dense, Modified MIT), Mixtral MoE variants, Codestral
  • Strengths: Performance per parameter, 77.6% SWE-bench Verified (Medium 3.5), EU hosting
  • License: Apache 2.0 (Small 4 / Large 3); Modified MIT with monthly revenue cap (Medium 3.5) — verify which applies to your use

Download Links

  • Official Mistral
  • Hugging Face
  • Ollama

5. Gemma 4 (Google)

Overview

The biggest licensing story of the year in open weights: Gemma 4 moved to Apache 2.0, replacing Gemma 3's custom terms (note: Apache 2.0 is paired with a separate Prohibited Use Policy — not a bare license). The lineup is now five sizes, including an MoE tier that activates only 3.8B parameters. Same lightweight, multimodal, multilingual strengths — 140+ languages — now with far less legal friction.

Key Specifications

  • Sizes: E2B, E4B, 12B Unified, 26B MoE (A4B — 3.8B active), 31B Dense
  • Variants: Pre-trained (base) and instruction-tuned; ShieldGemma 2 is a companion safety model, not a Gemma 4 variant
  • Strengths: Runs on consumer hardware and mobile, natively multimodal (video, images; audio on E2B/E4B), 140+ languages, 128K–256K context
  • License: Apache 2.0 (Gemma 4) plus the Gemma Prohibited Use Policy; older Gemma 1–3 used custom Gemma Terms

Download Links

  • Google AI Studio
  • Kaggle
  • Hugging Face
  • Ollama

6. gpt-oss (OpenAI)

Overview

OpenAI's first open-weight language models since GPT-2: two MoE models under Apache 2.0, natively quantized in MXFP4 with 128K context. The 120B model reaches near-parity with o4-mini on reasoning benchmarks; the 20B matches or exceeds o3-mini. For teams standardized on OpenAI's API formats, these are the natural self-hosted fallback — compatible with the Responses API and the open-sourced Harmony prompt format.

Key Specifications

  • Sizes: gpt-oss-120b (117B total / 5.1B active), gpt-oss-20b (21B total / 3.6B active)
  • Strengths: OpenAI-compatible behavior, permissive license, exposed chain-of-thought, tool use
  • License: Apache 2.0

Download Links

  • Hugging Face
  • Ollama

7. Phi (Microsoft)

Overview

The small-model specialist: Phi models (1.3B–15B, MIT) trained on curated textbook-quality synthetic data, designed for memory/compute-constrained and latency-bound scenarios. A full reasoning line exists (Phi-4-reasoning, mini reasoning, and a 15B multimodal reasoning variant).

Key Specifications

  • Sizes: 1.3B to 15B
  • Strengths: Best-in-class tiny models, reasoning variants, runs on phones and laptops
  • License: MIT

Download Links

  • Hugging Face
  • Ollama

8. Kimi K3 (Moonshot AI) & GLM-4.7 (Z.ai)

Overview

Two newer entrants worth tracking. Moonshot's Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter MoE (896 experts, 104B activated) with 1M-token context and native multimodal input, positioned as an agentic long-horizon coding model — under a custom Kimi K3 License, not a permissive OSI license. Z.ai's GLM-4.7 is a coding-focused open model (73.8% SWE-bench Verified) with strong multilingual coding performance, released under MIT.

Rule of thumb: the smallest model that passes your eval wins. Don't reach for 70B when a well-chosen 8B clears the bar — it's cheaper, faster, and fits more hardware.

Choosing the Right Model

For General Use

Llama 4 (Maverick-class) or Qwen3 (235B-class) — best balance of capability and ecosystem. Llama if you want maximum community support; Qwen if you want Apache 2.0 certainty.

For Coding

Qwen3-Coder and DeepSeek variants are purpose-built for code generation, debugging, and agentic coding workflows. GLM-4.7 is a strong MIT-licensed alternative.

For Limited Hardware

Gemma 4 (E4B) or Phi (3–4B) — run on consumer GPUs, high-end CPUs, even mobile. Now both under permissive licenses.

For Long Documents

Qwen3-Coder (1M via extrapolation), DeepSeek V4 (1M), or Kimi K3 (1M) — built for entire codebases and book-length context.

For Math and Reasoning

DeepSeek-R1 — still the open reference for step-by-step reasoning and proofs.

For Commercial Products

Prefer MIT / Apache 2.0 families (Qwen, DeepSeek, Gemma 4, gpt-oss, Phi, GLM-4.7, Mistral Small/Large). Treat Llama's Community License, Mistral Medium's revenue-capped terms, and Kimi K3's custom license as requiring legal review.

Important Considerations

  • Hardware: 70B+ models typically need 48GB+ VRAM or significant RAM for CPU inference; quantized 7–8B models run on a single consumer GPU
  • Quantization: GGUF/AWQ quants run on far less hardware with minimal quality loss — always check for quantized releases before buying GPUs
  • Licenses: "open" is not one thing. Apache 2.0/MIT are safe defaults; custom community licenses may restrict commercial scale or require gating
  • Freshness: Open-weight releases now ship monthly. Pin versions in production; re-evaluate quarterly
